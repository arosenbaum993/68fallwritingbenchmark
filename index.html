Question: 1
SCENARIO AND SOURCES

Scenario: Your district is considering requiring every middle school student to complete community-service hours before finishing eighth grade. The superintendent has asked students to study the issue and recommend whether the requirement should be adopted.

Research Question: Should students in grades 6-8 be required to complete community service?

You have three sources about student service. Review all three sources, then construct an argumentative text that answers the research question. Use the Notepad tool or scratch paper to plan.

Source #1

Read the informational article about service and learning.

Learning Beyond the Classroom

Practice source by Elena Ruiz

1Community service asks people to use their time and effort to improve the place where they live. Students might stock shelves at a food pantry, collect litter from a park, read with younger children, or help an animal shelter organize supplies.

2These projects can teach skills that are difficult to practice in a workbook. A student helping at a community garden must listen to directions, solve unexpected problems, and complete a job even when it becomes tiring. Students also meet people whose experiences may be different from their own.

3Service becomes most meaningful when students connect action with reflection. After a project, students can write about the need they observed, the choices they made, and what they would change next time. That reflection turns an activity into a learning experience.

4A requirement may also ensure that every student has an opportunity to serve, not only students who already belong to clubs or have families with volunteer connections. Schools can introduce students to local organizations and help them discover interests that may lead to future courses or careers.

5However, simply counting hours does not guarantee learning. A strong program offers several project choices, prepares students for their work, and asks them to reflect afterward. The quality of the experience matters more than a signature on a form.

Source #2

Read the commentary about problems with mandatory service.

When Volunteering Is Not Voluntary

Practice source by Marcus Chen

1Helping others is valuable, but schools should be cautious about making community service a graduation requirement. The word volunteer usually describes a person who chooses to help. When service becomes mandatory, students may focus on completing hours instead of understanding a community need.

2A requirement can also affect students unevenly. Some students care for siblings after school. Others work, participate in athletics, lack transportation, or live far from approved service sites. Giving every student the same number of hours does not give every student the same chance to complete them.

3Schools would need staff members to approve sites, track forms, solve transportation problems, and confirm that hours were completed honestly. Time spent managing paperwork could reduce the time counselors and teachers have for other student needs.

4Schools can encourage service without attaching it to promotion or graduation. They could hold service days during school, recognize students who volunteer, or allow service projects to replace certain assignments. These choices would make service accessible while preserving student choice.

5Community involvement should grow from purpose, not pressure. If a school creates useful opportunities and explains why they matter, more students may decide to participate willingly.

Source #3

Review the proposed district plan.

Middle School Community Partnership Proposal

Practice planning document

Proposed Requirements

Hours: 12 total during grades 6-8
Choices: campus, neighborhood, or approved home-based projects
School support: two service days each year during school hours
Transportation: provided for district-sponsored projects
Reflection: one short written or recorded reflection per project
Alternatives: students with work, caregiving, health, or transportation barriers may design an equivalent school-based project

1The proposal spreads twelve hours across three school years rather than requiring students to complete them all at once. Students could work individually or with a team. Projects might include tutoring, environmental cleanup, donation drives, oral-history interviews, or creating materials for a nonprofit organization.

2Each school would appoint a service coordinator. The coordinator would publish project choices, verify completion, and help students select accessible alternatives. Teachers could connect reflections to ELA, science, social studies, or career lessons.

3Supporters say the plan answers fairness concerns because students can complete hours during the school day and choose alternative projects. Critics respond that the plan still requires staff time and that students may treat reflection as another assignment. The school board must decide whether the educational benefits justify those costs.

Research Question: Should students in grades 6-8 be required to complete community service?

Grades 6-8 Writer's Checklist

In my well-written text, I made sure to:

  • introduce the topic and capture the reader's attention.
  • clearly state my claim or position.
  • organize my reasons and evidence effectively.
  • support my reasons with evidence from multiple sources.
  • credit the sources I used.
  • explain how each piece of evidence supports my claim.
  • address an opposing claim.
  • use varied transitions to connect ideas.
  • provide a strong conclusion.
  • correct errors in spelling, capitalization, punctuation, usage, and grammar.
